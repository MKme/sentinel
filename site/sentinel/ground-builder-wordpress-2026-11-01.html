ONE SENSOR IS A START. A NETWORK IS WHAT YOU MAKE IT.

Your next field tool
can start on your workbench.

Sentinel is an open-source ground-sensor project with firmware, wiring references and readable packet examples. Build a stationary sensing node, give it a recognizable name, and bring its reports into your field workflow.

01 / BUILD

A sensing post

Use the XIAO ESP32-S3 + Wio-SX1262 Presence firmware for passive Wi-Fi activity detection, RCWL-0516 motion cues and optional status reports. Mount it at a gate, equipment site or stationary observation point.

02 / CONNECT

A mesh participant

Send readable alerts over your configured Meshtastic channel. The radio also forwards eligible mesh packets according to its role, hop limit and channel settings. Plan antenna placement and test the path at your site.

03 / CREATE

Your own sensor

Start with the ESP32 PIR example and a documented T=11 Sentinel report. Adapt the code for a contact switch, temperature probe, battery monitor or a combination that fits your project.

The Presence firmware supplies motion and Wi-Fi sensing. Custom contact, temperature and battery builds use your chosen hardware and adaptations to the standalone examples.

LOCAL DETECTION / MESH DELIVERY

Something changes.
Your node reports it.

Sentinel passively listens for qualifying Wi-Fi transmissions, applies your signal-strength threshold and reports activity over LoRa through Meshtastic. Add an RCWL-0516 motion sensor to combine radio activity with a motion cue at a stationary node.

01 / Listen

Scan your configured Wi-Fi channels.

02 / Filter

Apply signal thresholds, duplicate filtering and reporting intervals.

03 / Correlate

Combine motion and Wi-Fi activity within your selected time window.

04 / Report

Example messages. Wi-Fi detection indicates qualifying radio activity. The status counter tracks cumulative deduplicated observations since boot. Use the alert as a cue to review the situation.

Set it up locally.

Join the node's SENTINEL-xxxx setup network during its boot window and open 192.168.4.1. Choose a label, scan settings, motion timing and mesh output. Save, start detection and confirm the alert on a receiving node.

Know what it is doing.

USB diagnostics at 115200 baud show signal strength, Wi-Fi channel, motion transitions, scanner health and mesh-submission decisions. Configure radio region, modem preset, role and channel keys with the Meshtastic app.

FROM YOUR WORKBENCH TO YOUR OPERATING PICTURE

Make your hardware
part of X Suite.

The standalone Arduino examples show the complete path from a sensor input to a T=11 Sentinel packet. XTOC and XCOM decode those reports, giving your own hardware a common language with the tools your team uses.

BUILD / REPORT

Start with one PIR.

The clear-packet demo reads a motion input and prints a complete report over USB. Set your node ID, label and fixed coordinates, then import the line through Comms → Import/Export in XTOC or XCOM.

RECEIVE / REVIEW

Put the node in context.

Review decoded reports in Sentinel and show reports with configured coordinates on the Tactical Map Sentinel layer. Stable labels help your team recognize which sensor needs attention.

CONFIGURE / RESPOND

Make readings useful.

Configure XTOC triggers for labels, time conditions, temperature, battery values and input/output masks. Match each rule to the reports your custom node sends.

The Presence firmware sends readable mesh alerts; the standalone examples demonstrate direct T=11 import. Choose the build and transport that match your workflow.

YOUR CLASSIFIER. SENTINEL TRANSPORT.

Your Pi sees it.
Your team receives it.

Connect a Raspberry Pi or another companion computer over USB. Run your own camera and recognition software, then use the supplied generic sender to pass an object label, confidence, count and observation ID to Sentinel. The node sends the compact observation as a T=11 report over the private mesh.

01 / Observe

Your classifier produces an object or event observation.

02 / Submit

Your code sends the documented compact input over USB.

03 / Relay

Sentinel carries the label, confidence, count and observation age.

04 / Review

XTOC displays the observation and evaluates your enabled rules.

Companion observations explicitly mark location unavailable. Images and recognition software stay on your companion computer; the mesh carries the compact report.

Choose the observations that deserve attention.

The optional Sentinel Object Observation Alerts package in XPASS provides configurable SOA trigger templates and local plugin examples. In XTOC, configure the source, label, confidence and location-quality filters, then enable the rules you need. Use an updated receiver that supports the observation quality fields.
